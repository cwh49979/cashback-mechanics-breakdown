## The mechanic in about 40 seconds

Merchants pay ShopBack a referral commission when a click-through turns into a real sale. ShopBack keeps part of it and passes the rest to you as cashback. That's the whole business model, and it's why the account itself costs nothing.

In practice:

1. You open the ShopBack app, site, or browser extension
2. You search the store you were going to buy from anyway
3. You tap through to that store
4. You check out as normal, without wandering off to another tab or app

Step 3 is the one that matters. Cashback attribution runs on a tracking tag created at that click. Skip it and there is nothing to attribute, which is the single most common reason people report "it didn't track."

## What you'll actually earn, and where the big numbers live

Base rates at most merchants sit somewhere between 1% and 5%. Some categories climb higher, and every platform's marketing pages will show you a much larger number, because promotional rates are where the headline figures come from.

Ranked by how much they move the needle for a normal household:

**Travel.** This is where the per-transaction value is highest, and where forgetting to click through hurts most. Hotel and flight bookings through partner OTAs can pay several percent on a four-figure total. ShopBack's own US blog documented a year where travel accounted for US$245 of US$620 earned, from four trips. Travel cashback also takes the longest to confirm, because it doesn't clear until after the trip, not after the booking.

**Sale windows.** Double-date sales (9.9, 11.11) and Black Friday are when rates get bumped across participating merchants. The "up to X% cashback" banners you'll see are ceilings on specific stores during specific windows, not a flat rate.

**Large one-off purchases.** A laptop, a mattress, a big appliance. A single 4% on a $1,500 purchase beats six months of grocery-level cashback.

**Everyday retail.** Small and steady. A $6 delivery order earning 18 cents is technically correct and psychologically annoying.

**Amazon is the cautionary tale.** The Amazon rate has been cut more than once, in more than one market, and in the US it was reduced from a percentage payout to a flat structure capped at a handful of small rewards per month. If Amazon cashback is your entire reason for signing up, check the current terms on the merchant page before you assume it's still there.

## Pending, Confirmed, withdrawable: the part nobody reads

Your cashback doesn't appear as spendable money. It goes through three states, and knowing which one you're in saves a lot of unnecessary support tickets.

- **Pending.** Shows up in about 48 hours after purchase. The merchant is still inside its return window, so the amount can change or vanish if you return something.
- **Confirmed.** The merchant has validated the sale. Retail purchases usually clear within a few weeks. Travel doesn't clear until after the trip happens.
- **Withdrawable.** Confirmed cashback above your market's minimum, sent to your bank or e-wallet.

Withdrawal rules are set per market, not globally. Two documented examples:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Singapore dropped DBS PayLah! as a withdrawal option in December 2023, and PayNow has since been added alongside bank transfer. Other markets set their own local thresholds and rails, and the US runs PayPal. Check the withdrawal page in your own account before you plan around a payout date, because these are the numbers that change most often.

## Is Plus actually worth the fee?

Run the arithmetic instead of the vibes. The documented everyday uplift for Plus members has been around an extra 0.5 percentage points above the public rate at eligible merchants. Against a S$1.99 monthly fee, that means you'd need to route roughly S$400 of qualifying spend through ShopBack each month just to break even on the rate bump alone.

That's a real threshold. Most casual shoppers won't clear it in an average month. Where the maths flips is if you're planning a large purchase, a travel booking, or spending heavily during a double-date sale, and the boosted rate applies to the merchant you were already using. Subscribe, make the booking, decide whether to keep it after the free first month. There's no cancellation penalty, which is the main reason the risk is low.

Priority customer support is listed as a benefit too. There's no published response-time SLA attached to it, so treat it as a nice-to-have rather than the reason to pay.

## What silently kills a cashback session

Almost every "ShopBack ripped me off" story traces back to one of these. None of them are exotic.

- **Ad blockers.** They strip the affiliate tracking cookie. Whitelist ShopBack and the merchant, or shop through the mobile app instead.
- **Third-party promo codes.** A code from a coupon aggregator can overwrite ShopBack's affiliate tag with its own. Only use codes listed on ShopBack's own merchant page.
- **Switching browsers or tabs mid-session.** The tracking tag lives in the browser session that created it.
- **Clicking through on desktop, then finishing in the retailer's app.** The cookie doesn't transfer.
- **Starting at the retailer and remembering ShopBack afterwards.** Attribution windows close. There's no fixing this one retroactively.

The browser extension solves most of this on desktop, because it detects when you land on a partner merchant and offers a one-click activation. Reports from people tracking their own monthly earnings consistently show it as the single change that turns the platform from an occasional tool into a routine. If you shop on mobile, the equivalent is simply opening the app first.

When something genuinely doesn't track, you're not stuck. Go to your click activity, find the store and date, and file a missing cashback report with the order number. Resolution takes time but it does work.

Nobody wins on every merchant, and rates swap leaders week to week. The pragmatic setup is two or three accounts and a check before each purchase.

| TopCashback | UK and India, known for passing through a high share of commission | Bank, PayPal, Venmo, gift cards | Very low, often around £1 or ₹1 |
| Ibotta / Fetch | Grocery and receipt scanning, which portals don't cover | In-app redemption | Varies |

One absolute rule: you cannot stack two cashback portals on one purchase. Attribution follows whichever affiliate cookie was set last before checkout, so only one pays. You can hold accounts at all of them; you just have to pick one per order.

For Asian and Australian shoppers, ShopBack tends to have the deeper local merchant list, including travel partners that aren't available on US-centric platforms. For US shoppers, Rakuten's longer track record and quarterly schedule are the usual default, though ShopBack's US differentiators are the Snaps receipt scanner and the Play games layer, both app-only.
